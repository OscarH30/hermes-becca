# Cold Email Copywriting Frameworks

Frameworks beat templates, they teach thinking patterns, not copy-paste shortcuts.

**Read this first.** The examples below are long, to show each pattern. Your real first email is under 90 words, plain text, with no links, one small ask, and only true facts. Use a framework for the order of ideas, then cut the example down. Never copy a result, a customer name, or a number from these examples. They are illustrations, not facts about your business.

## PAS: Problem, Agitate, Solution (default)

**Structure:** Identify pain then Amplify consequences then Present solution + soft CTA.
**Best for:** Problem-aware but not solution-aware prospects. The workhorse framework.

> Most VP Sales at companies your size spend a lot of each week on manual CRM reporting. That is time not spent coaching reps, and it often means inaccurate forecasts reaching leadership. We built a tool that auto-generates CRM reports in real time. [A real result from your business facts, or leave this line out.] Worth a quick look?

## BAB: Before, After, Bridge

**Structure:** Current painful situation then Ideal future then Your product as the bridge.
**Best for:** Transformation-driven offers with clear before/after. Emotional decision-makers.

> Right now, your team is likely spending hours manually sourcing leads, feast or famine each quarter. Imagine qualified leads arriving daily on autopilot, reps spending more of their time selling. That's what our platform does. [A real result from your business facts, or leave this line out.] Worth a quick look?

## QVC: Question, Value, CTA

**Structure:** Targeted pain question then Brief value then Direct next step.
**Best for:** C-suite prospects who prefer brevity. Qualify interest immediately.

> Are your SDRs spending more time researching than selling? We help sales teams automate prospect research so reps focus on conversations. [A real result from your business facts, or leave this line out.] Worth a quick look?

## AIDA: Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

**Structure:** Hook/stat then Address specific challenge then Social proof/outcome then Clear CTA.
**Best for:** Data-driven prospects, high-ticket pitches with strong stats.

> Companies in [your industry] often lose leads to slow, manual outreach. Given {{Company}}'s growth this quarter, pipeline velocity is likely top of mind. [A real result from your business facts, or leave this line out.] Worth a quick look?

## PPP: Praise, Picture, Push

**Structure:** Genuine compliment then How things could be better then Gentle push to action.
**Best for:** Senior prospects who respond to relationship-building. Requires genuine trigger.

> Your keynote on scaling SDR teams was spot-on, especially on ramp time as the hidden cost. What if you could cut that in half? Our in-inbox coach helps new reps write effective emails from day one with real-time scoring. Worth a quick look?

## Star-Story-Solution

**Structure:** Introduce character (customer) then Tell challenge narrative then Reveal results.
**Best for:** Strong customer success stories. Humanizes the pitch.

> Last year, a VP Sales at a small startup had a small team competing against a much bigger rival. Her team was getting outnumbered. [Describe what changed and the real result from your business facts, or leave this line out.] Want me to send the short version for {{Company}}?

## SCQ: Situation, Complication, Question

**Structure:** Current reality then Complicating challenge then Question that speaks to need then Optional answer.
**Best for:** Consultative selling. Mirrors how professionals present to leadership.

> Your team doubled this year. That usually means onboarding is eating into selling time. How are you handling ramp for new hires?

## ACCA: Awareness, Comprehension, Conviction, Action

**Structure:** Contrarian hook then Explain benefit simply then Provide proof then Strong CTA.
**Best for:** Analytical buyers who need evidence (engineers, CFOs, ops leaders).

> Most sales teams measure rep activity. Some teams measure rep efficiency instead. [A real result from your business facts, or leave this line out.] Worth seeing how?

## 3C's (Berman)

**Structure:** Compliment then Case Study then CTA.
**Best for:** Agency/services cold outreach. Case study does the heavy lifting.

> Big fan of [Company]. We just built an app for [Competitor] that does XYZ. I have a few more ideas. Interested?

## Mouse Trap (Lavender/Will Allred)

**Structure:** Observation + Binary value-prop question. 1 to 2 sentences total.
**Best for:** Maximum brevity. Impulsive reply based on curiosity.

> Looks like you're hiring reps. Would it be helpful to get a more granular look at how they're ramping on email?

## Justin Michael Method

**Structure:** Trigger/Pain then Solution hint then Binary ask. 1 to 3 sentences, no intro.
**Best for:** High-velocity SDR teams. Mobile-optimized. Deliberately polarizing.

Spend max 1 minute on personalization. Use industry/persona-level signals. For top-tier prospects, quote their own words from interviews, they almost always respond.

## Vanilla Ice Cream (Lavender)

**Structure:** Observation then Problem/Insight then Credibility then Solution then Call-to-Conversation.
**Best for:** Universal "base" framework that works everywhere. Five parts.

## PASTOR (Ray Edwards)

**Structure:** Problem then Amplify then Story then Testimony then Offer then Response.
**Best for:** Longer-form or multi-email sequences. Consulting, education, complex B2B services. Each element can be developed across separate touches.
